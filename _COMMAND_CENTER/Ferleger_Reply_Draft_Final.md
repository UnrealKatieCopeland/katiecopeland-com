I want to clarify the core theory of the case, because I believe my initial framing in the packet may have muddied the water. This is not a case about whether the trial court should have provided a CART expert, or whether the failure to do so was an abuse of discretion. 

This is a structural claim under 28 C.F.R. § 35.107, based on the County's own written admissions that it possesses none of the required administrative infrastructure to process an accommodation request in the first place.

Here is how the record answers the three main concerns you raised:

**1. The "Capable Lawyer" Defense and the Expert Question**
You noted that the court viewed me as a capable lawyer and that proving the necessity of a specific accommodation (like CART) would require an expert. 

The structural claim does not require proving that CART was the *correct* accommodation. It requires proving that the County had no mechanism to make *any* determination. Under *Luke v. Texas*, 46 F.4th 301 (5th Cir. 2022), the lack of meaningful access "is itself the harm."

Furthermore, the "capable lawyer" defense is contradicted by the medical record and the proceedings themselves:
*   **SSA Adjudication:** The Social Security Administration has adjudicated me totally disabled with an onset date of June 8, 2023—before the first trial and eleven months before the State argued I was "talking disabled when it's convenient" (RR Vol. 20 at 236).
*   **Medical Collapses on the Record:** The record documents a medical collapse requiring emergency evaluation at the August 17, 2023 trial (RR Vols. 11–12), and a cognitive collapse on the record on May 6, 2024 ("I just lost — I have to start my brain back," RR Vol. 19 at 31–32). The court proceeded with the morning of day two in my absence; I returned after lunch with my personal care attendant (RR Vol. 20).

**2. The County's Written Admissions**
The claim that the County lacks a coordinator or grievance process is not an inference; it is their stated position.
*   On April 1, 2026, the Assistant County Administrator stated in writing: "I am not aware of how each court handles their request once they get notified."
