# Targeted Outreach Strategy: ADA Title II Court Access
**Prepared for Katie Copeland**
**Date:** July 27, 2026

This document outlines a targeted outreach strategy to engage disability rights practitioners, scholars, and organizations regarding the pending Supreme Court application in *Copeland v. Tarrant County* (No. 25A1368). The strategy is designed to leverage the "open question" framing—specifically the administrative void surrounding 28 C.F.R. § 35.107—to secure amicus support, co-counsel, or strategic referrals.

## I. The Strategic Frame: The Administrative Void

To engage sophisticated practitioners, the outreach must immediately signal that this case presents a clean, unresolved legal question with national implications. 

**The Core Message:**
While *Tennessee v. Lane* established that Title II applies to state courts, the granular application of 28 C.F.R. § 35.107 (requiring an ADA coordinator and grievance procedure) to court-participation accommodations remains a distinct legal frontier. Most circuits treat the absence of these administrative mechanisms as mere evidence of deliberate indifference rather than a standalone violation. *Copeland v. Tarrant County* presents this open question with a pristine record: the County's written admission that it has zero compliance infrastructure, combined with documented medical collapses during federal adjudication.

**Key Vocabulary to Use:**
*   **The Administrative Void:** The complete absence of § 35.107 infrastructure.
*   **Court-Participation Accommodations:** The specific frontier of Title II enforcement.
*   **Structural Noncompliance:** Framing the issue as a systemic failure, not just an individual denial.
*   **The Shadow Docket:** Acknowledging the current posture (Rule 22.4 renewal pending before Justice Sotomayor) while highlighting the urgency.
