## EMAIL BODY

Dear colleagues,

I am writing to flag a pending Fifth Circuit case that presents a question no circuit has squarely decided — and to ask for one of three things, the easiest of which is a single name.

**The open question:** Do § 35.107's administrative obligations — designated ADA coordinator, intake process, grievance procedure — extend to court-participation accommodations? *Copeland v. Tarrant County*, No. 26-10389 (5th Cir.), presents that question in the context of a Texas county courthouse with no court-user ADA infrastructure of any kind.

**What makes this record unusual:** The central facts are not in dispute. Tarrant County's own open-records response (CDA-2026-897, April 21, 2026) states it was "unable to locate any responsive information" regarding an ADA coordinator, self-evaluation, grievance procedure, interactive process, or written determinations — all five items required under 28 C.F.R. Part 35 since January 26, 1992. The County's own Assistant Administrator wrote, in a dated email: *"I am not aware of how each court handles their request once they get notified."* That email is in the appendix.

**The procedural posture:** The district court dismissed at screening, before any defendant was served. The Fifth Circuit denied emergency interim relief — but Judge Jennifer Haynes stated she would grant it, twice, in two separate orders. Her dissent is in the record. The merits appeal is pending. An emergency application is before Justice Sotomayor (No. 25A1368, refiled July 1, 2026 — pending).

**The precedent potential:** A holding that § 35.107 reaches court-participation accommodations would require every public entity operating a courtroom to maintain what Tarrant County admits it lacks. The courts that enforce the ADA would themselves have to comply with it.

**The litigant:** I am a Texas attorney (Bar No. 24086056), ABA-published on courtroom accessibility, and federally adjudicated totally disabled since June 8, 2023 — before the first trial and every collapse. The lawyer and the litigant are the same person. The record was built to be used.

**Three ways to help — easiest first:**

1. **One referral name.** If you know a disability-rights attorney, organization, or clinic that handles Title II court-access claims, a single introduction is the most valuable thing anyone can offer. No commitment required beyond the email.

2. **Amicus participation.** FRAP 29 amicus participation is open in the Fifth Circuit. The § 35.107 question is ripe for a circuit-level statement from the bar.

3. **Co-counsel or consultation.** The Fifth Circuit appeal is the live vehicle. A standalone trial-level action with a fully built record is also viable. Fee-shifting is available under 42 U.S.C. § 12205. A one-hour consult is welcomed with no further obligation.
