Doctrinal Framework

The structural failure documented above implicates core ADA Title II and due process doctrines.

  • Tennessee v. Lane (2004): Access to courts is the core of Title II. The right to meaningful participation in judicial proceedings is a fundamental due process right.
  • Crawford v. Hinds County (5th Cir. 2021) & Luke v. Texas (5th Cir. 2022): These cases frame the structural-failure and participation-injury framework within the Fifth Circuit, establishing that systemic deficiencies can constitute actionable Title II violations.
  • Strife (5th Cir. 2025): Establishes that unjustifiable delay in processing or granting accommodations operates effectively as a denial.
  • A.J.T. v. Osseo (9th Cir. 2025): Clarifies that no heightened intent showing is required for structural failures; the failure to maintain required infrastructure is sufficient.
The Central Thesis: "A right without a route is not access." When a public entity fails to provide the administrative structure mandated by 28 C.F.R. § 35.107, it effectively forecloses the substantive rights protected by Title II.
