U.S. Supreme Court · 2004
Tennessee v. Lane

Title II reaches state courts, and access to the courts is a fundamental right Congress may protect.

2
Fifth Circuit, en banc · 2011
Frame v. City of Arlington
657 F.3d 215

Regulations that “simply apply Title II's substantive ban” are enforceable through Title II's private right of action.

3
Fifth Circuit · 2022
Luke v. Texas
46 F.4th 301

A county does not enjoy sovereign immunity. Not being able to understand a court hearing is “by definition, a lack of meaningful access.” That lack “is itself the harm,” and no bad outcome need be alleged. A public entity may not force someone else to supply the access “for which the entity is responsible.”

4
Fifth Circuit · 2022
Crawford v. Hinds County
Board of Supervisors

A disabled person has standing to enjoin courthouse-accessibility barriers; a risk of future exclusion is enough.

5
U.S. Supreme Court · 2025
A.J.T. v. Osseo Area Schools
Independent School District No. 279

No heightened “bad faith or gross misjudgment” showing is required. Ordinary standards apply.

6
Fifth Circuit · 2025 · Title I, by analogy
Strife v. Aldine ISD
138 F.4th 237

A request triggers the duty to engage, and an unjustified delay can itself be a failure to accommodate — with no adverse action required.

The last question
No circuit has squarely answered it

Does a public entity discharge its § 12132 obligation to a court user by routing every accommodation request to the presiding judge — leaving no one answerable for whether a disabled person can participate?

What a favorable holding would do
01
Name a responsible party

Every county operating a courtroom would have to identify someone answerable for participation access — the thing Tarrant County says does not exist.

02
Make the request reviewable

An accommodation decision would be made, in writing, before the proceeding — not reconstructed afterward from a loss.

03
Close the loop

No county could route a request to the bench and call the matter closed. Luke already forbids offloading access onto someone else.

The line is nearly finished.
It needs one more case — and better hands than mine alone.
