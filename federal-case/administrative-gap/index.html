The Open Question — Fifth Circuit

Can a public entity satisfy Title II simply by routing every court-participation accommodation request to the presiding judge — with no coordinator, no written determination, and no grievance pathway?

The Fifth Circuit has never answered this question. The County's own written admissions present it on a clean record.

Already Decided

  • Tennessee v. Lane, 541 U.S. 509 (2004) — Title II applies to court proceedings
  • Crawford v. Hinds County (5th Cir.) — entity-level Title II obligations
  • Luke v. Texas (5th Cir.) — access to courts under Title II
  • 28 C.F.R. § 35.107 — coordinator and grievance required since 1992

Open Question

  • Whether Title II's administrative-infrastructure obligations reach court-participation accommodations
  • Whether routing requests to a judge satisfies the entity's § 35.107 obligations
  • Whether denial of accommodation without a written entity-level determination triggers § 12132 liability
  • Whether the "closed loop" exhaustion trap constitutes interference under § 12203
