Executive Snapshot

This appeal asks whether a state court system can impose binding legal consequences—such as dismissals, struck pleadings, and sanctions—through proceedings that afford a disabled litigant no accessible, reviewable pathway to seek ADA Title II accommodations. The immediate objective is to secure appellate counsel to litigate this question in the Fifth Circuit before the underlying harms render the appeal moot.

The Core Issue: A Right Without a Route

The operative theory is that Tarrant County lacks an identifiable ADA Title II / § 504 accommodation pathway or grievance review structure. When a disabled court user cannot find the doorway to request an accommodation, they cannot meaningfully participate. When they cannot participate, they face default consequences. And when they attempt to appeal those consequences, the lack of an accommodation record is used against them.

“The question is not whether I should prevail in every underlying case. It is whether I may be made to lose them before any court decides whether I was given a lawful way to participate. A right without a route is not access.” — Emergency Application to SCOTUS
Why this matters for Title II Doctrine

This case presents an opportunity for the Fifth Circuit to clarify that ADA Title II requires more than a theoretical promise of access; it requires the administrative structure—the coordinators and grievance procedures mandated by regulation—to make that access real. A state cannot use the consequences of an inaccessible proceeding to foreclose review of the accessibility failure itself.
