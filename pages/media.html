Long Bio (300+ words)

Katie Copeland, J.D., is a Texas attorney, author, and access architect specializing in disability rights, family court reform, institutional accountability, and systems change. Based in Dallas–Fort Worth, she develops public-facing legal frameworks and resources on ADA Title II compliance, meaningful access to courts, due process, and the intersection of disability, family law, and judicial accountability.

Her work blends rigorous legal analysis with lived experience, translating complex access failures into clear questions, practical tools, and actionable frameworks for systemic reform. Katie's approach is dialectical—holding complexity rather than collapsing it—and grounded in the principle that access is not a courtesy. It is the doorway to due process.

Publications & Recognition: Katie's article "Access Is Not Advantage" was published in the American Bar Association journal in March 2026 and was amplified by the ABA Commission on Disability Rights for its foundational argument that disability accommodations in court are legal requirements, not judicial favors. She is the author of the forthcoming manifesto The Invisible Barrier: Disability, Due Process, and the Architecture of Exclusion in American Courts, which exposes how America's state courts systematically fail to comply with ADA Title II and provides a framework for holding them accountable.

Frameworks & Tools: Katie created The Copeland Questions™, a diagnostic framework that reveals exactly where a court's ADA Title II obligations were violated. She is also the founder of the Systems Justice Institute, dedicated to public education, court access reform, and advocacy.

Speaking & Engagement: Katie speaks nationally on ADA Title II compliance, meaningful access to courts, family court reform, disability rights, and systems accountability. She is available for conferences, webinars, podcasts, and media interviews.
