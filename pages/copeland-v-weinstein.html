Copeland v. Weinstein Management Co., Inc. et al.

A Federal Disability-Housing Civil Rights Case in the Northern District of Texas

This page provides a plain-language public record for a federal disability-housing civil rights case filed pro se in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas, Fort Worth Division.

Read the Summary Share Your Experience View Documents

The Case in One Minute

Katie Copeland is a Texas attorney and disabled tenant who alleges that Weinstein Management Co., Inc. and related defendants failed to provide reasonable disability-related housing accommodations after receiving medical and documentary notice of disability-related needs.

According to the verified complaint, the evidence includes a fully favorable SSDI determination, contemporaneous treating-physician records, a contamination report from the landlord's own retained contractor, a written demand letter, and a 14-month multi-forum litigation history. The complaint alleges that the defendants' conduct implicated federal disability-housing protections, including the Fair Housing Act's reasonable-accommodation requirement and interference provision, the ADA's anti-interference provision, and federal declaratory-relief statutes.

Plain-language theory of the case: When a housing provider has notice that a tenant's disability affects housing access, the law does not require sympathy. It requires a meaningful accommodation process, non-interference, and access to housing on equal terms.

Why This Matters

This case matters because disability housing rights are not limited to ramps and parking spaces. They also include the right to request a reasonable change in rules, policies, practices, or services when that change may be necessary for a disabled person to use and enjoy housing.

Federal law recognizes that a refusal to make a necessary reasonable accommodation can be a form of housing discrimination. The Fair Housing Act also prohibits coercion, intimidation, threats, or interference with someone's exercise or enjoyment of fair-housing rights. The ADA contains a separate anti-interference provision protecting the exercise of ADA rights.

Legal Issue Plain-Language Meaning Why It Matters Here
Reasonable Accommodation A housing provider may need to adjust a rule, policy, practice, or service when necessary for equal housing access. The case alleges that medically supported accommodation needs were not meaningfully resolved.
Interference A person or entity may not interfere with someone's exercise of fair-housing rights. The case alleges that the response to protected requests created barriers rather than access.
ADA Anti-Interference The ADA protects people from coercion, intimidation, threats, or interference related to ADA rights. The case alleges disability-related rights were burdened after notice.
Declaratory Relief A federal court may declare the parties' legal rights. The case seeks clarity about obligations and rights going forward.

The Legal Questions

These questions are not abstract. They are the questions presented by the record in Copeland v. Weinstein Management Co., Inc. et al.

  1. When a disabled tenant provides medical documentation, what does a meaningful accommodation process require?
  2. When a landlord's own retained contractor documents contamination concerns, what obligations follow once disability-related housing access is at issue?
  3. When a tenant invokes federal disability-housing rights, where is the line between ordinary litigation defense and unlawful interference?
  4. When state-court litigation continues after a nonsuit intended to preserve federal claims, what federal relief is necessary to clarify rights and prevent ongoing harm?
  5. How many procedural doors can close before "access" becomes a word on paper rather than a right in practice?

About the Plaintiff

Katie Copeland is a Texas attorney, author, and disability-rights advocate whose work focuses on meaningful access to courts, disability accommodation, and the structural integrity of legal systems. Her 2026 article, "Access Is Not Advantage: Structural Integrity and Disability Accommodation in State Courts," was published through the American Bar Association Commission on Disability Rights.

In Copeland v. Weinstein Management Co., Inc. et al., she appears pro se as a disabled tenant and plaintiff asserting federal disability-housing civil rights claims. The public record includes a verified complaint, medical and documentary evidence, a pre-suit demand letter, a contamination report, and a multi-forum procedural history.
