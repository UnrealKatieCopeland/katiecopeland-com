The Question

When a disabled person needs accommodations in Tarrant County court, where do they go?

A courthouse is not accessible if the only door is invisible.

This case asks whether federal courts can abstain while a disabled court user faces ongoing legal consequences without an identified ADA Title II / § 504 court-access pathway.

The Closed Loop

Tarrant County's written responses to federal litigation reveal a systemic institutional failure:

  1. Ask the judge. Court accommodation requests are routed to individual judges, not through the County's ADA compliance system.
  2. If the judge denies or ignores it: The County says judges control their courtrooms and the County has "no control" over judicial ADA decisions.
  3. Then what? The disabled person has no clear ADA coordinator, no written accommodation determination, and no grievance route.

The County could not locate responsive records identifying:

  • A designated ADA Title II coordinator for courts
  • A written court-user accommodation process
  • A grievance procedure for denied accommodations
  • A self-evaluation of court-access barriers

That is not a policy disagreement. That is a documented institutional gap.

Why This Matters

For Tarrant County residents:

People enter Tarrant County courts as jurors, witnesses, tenants, parents, defendants, crime victims, protective-order applicants, debtors, and caregivers. If someone needs CART, extra processing time, remote access, breaks, accessible forms, or a written ruling, the public should know where that request goes. This is a civic infrastructure question.

For disability advocates:

Meaningful access to courts requires a visible route, not just a right on paper. This case clarifies that institutional responsibility cannot disappear through procedural routing.

For legal observers:

The Fifth Circuit denied emergency relief, but Judge Haynes twice indicated she would grant temporary relief or carry the issue with the merits panel. That signals serious judicial concern about the underlying question.

For everyone:

This case sits at the intersection of disability rights, court access, family law, poverty, and institutional power. It asks how institutions can erase people politely, one form at a time.

The Legal Arguments

The ADA Title II / § 504 Question:

Does the ADA require federal courts and court systems to maintain an identified, accessible pathway for court users to request accommodations, receive written determinations, and access grievance review?

The Institutional Responsibility Question:

When a court system routes accommodation requests to individual judges while disclaiming institutional responsibility, does that satisfy ADA Title II / § 504 obligations?

The Emergency Relief Question:

Is temporary injunctive relief warranted where a disabled court user faces ongoing proceedings, deadlines, sanctions, or enforcement consequences without an identified accommodation pathway?

The Abstention Question:

Can federal courts abstain from reviewing ADA court-access infrastructure while a disabled litigant faces ongoing legal consequences in state court?
